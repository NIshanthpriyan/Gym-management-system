1. Warm-Up

5-10 Minutes - Essential Before Every Session

Why Warm-Up? Warming up gradually raises your heart rate, increases blood flow to your muscles, and prepares joints for exercise. It reduces the risk of injury, improves performance, and mentally prepares you for the workout ahead.

Treadmill Walk

Cardio - Low Intensity

5 min 3-4 km/h
Walk at a slow, comfortable pace on the treadmill. Keep your back straight, arms swinging naturally, and breathe deeply. Gradually increase speed after 2 minutes to raise your heart rate gently. This primes your cardiovascular system and warms up leg muscles including quads, hamstrings, and calves.

Stationary Cycle

Cardio - Low Impact

5-7 min Light Resistance
Set the resistance to 1-2 and pedal at a steady, relaxed pace. Keep your back upright and grip handles lightly. An excellent low-impact alternative to treadmill walking - especially gentle on the knees. Engages your hip flexors, quads, and calves while elevating your heart rate progressively.

Jumping Jacks

Full Body - Dynamic

2 Sets 20 Reps 30s Rest
Stand with feet together, arms at your sides. Jump while spreading your feet shoulder-width apart and raising your arms overhead. Jump back to starting position. Keep the movement controlled and land softly. Jumping jacks are a full-body warm-up that raises heart rate quickly and activates coordination between upper and lower body.
2. Dynamic Stretching

Prepare Joints and Muscles for Resistance Training

Arm Circles

Shoulders - Mobility

2 x 10 each direction
Extend arms out to the sides at shoulder height. Make small circles, gradually increasing to large circles. Do 10 reps forward, then 10 reps backward. Benefits: Loosens the shoulder joint, improves range of motion, warms rotator cuff muscles, and prevents shoulder injuries during pressing and pulling exercises.

Leg Swings

Hips - Hamstrings - Balance

10 each leg - Front and Side
Hold a wall or rail for support. Swing one leg forward and back in a pendulum motion, gradually increasing the range. Repeat side-to-side. Benefits: Dynamically stretches the hamstrings and hip flexors, improves hip mobility, and activates the glutes - essential for squats and lunges.

Shoulder Rolls

Shoulders - Upper Back - Neck

10 Forward + 10 Backward
Stand or sit upright. Lift your shoulders up toward your ears, then roll them back and down in a smooth circular motion. Reverse direction. Benefits: Releases tension in the shoulder and upper back region, improves posture, and preps the traps and delts for pressing movements.

Hip Rotations

Hips - Core - Lower Back

10 each direction
Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, hands on hips. Make slow, large circles with your hips - 10 clockwise, then 10 counter-clockwise. Benefits: Lubricates the hip joints, activates core stabilisers, relieves stiffness in the lower back, and prepares the body for squatting and deadlifting patterns.

Walking Lunges

Quads - Glutes - Hip Flexors

2 x 10 steps
Step forward with your right foot, lower your left knee toward the floor (don't let it touch), then push up and step forward with your left foot. Keep your torso upright. Benefits: Activates quads, glutes, and hip flexors simultaneously while improving balance and coordination - a perfect final warm-up before leg day exercises.

Bodyweight Squats

Quads - Glutes - Hamstrings

3 Sets 10 Reps 60s Rest
Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Lower your body as if sitting back into a chair - keep chest up, knees tracking over toes, and weight in your heels. Go until thighs are parallel to the floor, then push back up. Key muscles: Quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings, and core. The squat is the king of lower-body exercises!

Push-ups / Knee Push-ups

Chest - Shoulders - Triceps

3 Sets 8 Reps 60s Rest
Start in a plank position (or on knees for modified). Lower your chest to the floor, elbows at 45 degrees, then push back up. Keep core tight throughout - don't let hips sag. Tip: If regular push-ups are too hard, do knee push-ups until you build upper body strength. Progress takes time!

Chest Press Machine

Chest - Anterior Deltoid - Triceps

3 Sets 10 Reps 75s Rest
Adjust the seat so handles are at chest level. Grip handles, push forward until arms are nearly extended - don't lock elbows. Slowly return to start. Beginner advantage: Machines guide your movement path, making them safer and easier to learn form on before transitioning to free weights.

Lat Pulldown

Latissimus Dorsi - Biceps - Rear Deltoids

3 Sets 10 Reps 75s Rest
Sit at the lat pulldown machine, gripping the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width. Pull the bar down to your upper chest, squeezing your lats at the bottom. Slowly release upward. Form tip: Lean back slightly (not excessively) and think about pulling your elbows toward your hips. Avoid swinging.

Seated Row

Mid-Back - Rhomboids - Biceps

3 Sets 10 Reps 75s Rest
Sit at the cable row machine, feet on the platform, knees slightly bent. Pull the handle toward your midsection, keeping elbows close to your body and squeezing your shoulder blades together. Slowly extend back out. Builds: A strong, thick back that improves posture and balances out your chest pressing work.

Shoulder Press Machine

Deltoids - Trapezius - Triceps

3 Sets 10 Reps 75s Rest
Adjust seat so handles are at shoulder height. Press upward until arms are almost fully extended. Lower slowly back to shoulder level. Form tip: Keep your core braced and avoid arching your lower back excessively. Builds broad, strong shoulders that improve your physique significantly.

Plank

Core - Transverse Abdominis - Lower Back

3 Sets 30 Seconds 60s Rest
Get into a forearm plank position - forearms on the floor, elbows below shoulders, body in a straight line from head to heels. Engage your core, squeeze your glutes, and breathe steadily. Don't let your hips drop or rise. Goal: Build core stability that supports every other exercise you perform in the gym.
4. Correct Form Tips

Follow These Rules to Train Safely and Effectively

🏋️
Use Light WeightsStart lighter than you think you need. Perfect form with light weight is always better than sloppy form with heavy weight.
🧍
Focus on Proper PostureKeep your spine neutral in most exercises. Avoid rounding your lower back or hunching your shoulders forward.
🐢
Control Every MovementLower weights slowly (2-3 seconds). Avoid using momentum - the muscle grows when it controls the load, not when it swings it.
🫴
Breathe CorrectlyExhale on the exertion (e.g. pushing or pulling). Inhale on the return phase. Never hold your breath during lifts.
✔
Never Sacrifice FormNever sacrifice proper execution or safety just to lift heavier weights (ego lifting). Progress gradually and safely.
🛑
Stop Immediately If Pain OccursMuscle burn is normal. Sharp, stabbing, or joint pain is NOT. Stop immediately and consult a trainer or physician before continuing that exercise.
5. Cool Down

5 Minutes - Always End Every Session With This

Why Cool Down? Cooling down helps your heart rate return to normal gradually, prevents blood pooling in the limbs, reduces muscle soreness (DOMS), and improves long-term flexibility. It is just as important as the workout itself!

Full Body Stretching

Flexibility - Recovery

5 min total
Hold each stretch for 20-30 seconds. Include: quad stretch, hamstring stretch, chest opener, tricep stretch, and child's pose. Breathe deeply into each stretch - never bounce or force the position. Static stretching after exercise is safe and highly beneficial.

Slow Walking

Heart Rate Recovery

2-3 min
Walk at a very slow pace on the treadmill or around the gym floor. This keeps blood moving and prevents dizziness that can occur from stopping exercise abruptly. Your heart rate should drop by 20-30 BPM during this phase.

Deep Breathing Exercises

Relaxation - Nervous System Recovery

2 min
Sit or lie down. Inhale deeply through your nose for 4 counts, hold for 2 counts, exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 counts. This activates your parasympathetic nervous system (rest and digest), reduces cortisol, and accelerates recovery. Do 8-10 breath cycles.

Intermediate Workout Plan

3-6 months experience - Push your limits safely

Barbell Squat

Quads - Glutes - Core

4 Sets8-10 Reps90s Rest
Use a barbell across your upper traps. Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out. Brace core, sit back and down, drive through heels. Increase weight by 2.5-5kg when you complete all reps with clean form.

Bench Press

Chest - Shoulders - Triceps

4 Sets8 Reps90s Rest
Lie on bench, grip bar slightly wider than shoulders. Lower to chest (touch, don't bounce), press up explosively. Keep feet flat on floor and maintain a slight arch in lower back. Always use a spotter.

Assisted Pull-Ups

Back - Biceps - Core

3 Sets6-8 Reps90s Rest
Hang from a bar, grip slightly wider than shoulders. Pull until chin clears the bar, lower with control. Use an assisted pull-up machine if needed. One of the best upper body exercises for building back width.

Romanian Deadlift

Hamstrings - Glutes - Lower Back

3 Sets10 Reps90s Rest
Hold dumbbells in front of thighs. Hinge at hips, pushing them back, lowering the weight along your legs until you feel a deep hamstring stretch. Drive hips forward to return. Never round your back.

Dumbbell Shoulder Press

Deltoids - Triceps

3 Sets10-12 Reps75s Rest
Sit on an upright bench, hold dumbbells at ear level. Press overhead until arms almost extend, lower slowly. Dumbbells challenge each arm independently, fixing strength imbalances that machines cannot address.

Ab Circuit

Core - Obliques - Lower Abs

3 Rounds45s each30s Rest
Cycle through: Crunches, Bicycle Crunches, Leg Raises, Russian Twists. 45 seconds per exercise, 30 seconds rest between them. Complete 3 full rounds for a comprehensive core session.
Advanced Workout Plan

12+ months experience - High intensity, high results

Deadlift

Full Body - Posterior Chain

5 Sets5 Reps2-3 min Rest
The king of all exercises. Bar over mid-foot, hips hinged, lats tight, drive through floor. Bracing the entire core like you're about to get punched is essential. Progress with 5x5 linear periodisation - add weight every session.

Front Squat

Quads - Core Dominant

4 Sets6 Reps2 min Rest
Bar rests on front deltoids, elbows high. Requires excellent ankle mobility and core bracing. More quad dominant than back squat and demands strict upright torso - exposes and fixes weaknesses that back squat can hide.

HIIT Circuit

Fat Loss - Conditioning

5 Rounds40s on / 20s off
Burpees, Box Jumps, Kettlebell Swings, Battle Ropes, Sprint. 40 seconds max effort, 20 seconds rest between exercises. 5 complete rounds with 90s rest between rounds. EPOC burns calories for 24-48 hours post-session.

Weighted Dips

Chest - Triceps - Anterior Delts

4 Sets8-10 Reps90s Rest
Use a dip belt with added plates. Lower until upper arms are parallel to floor, press back up. Lean slightly forward to emphasise chest. One of the best pressing movements for developing thickness in the lower chest and triceps.

Weighted Pull-Ups

Back Width - Biceps

4 Sets6-8 Reps90s Rest
Add a weight belt with plates. Dead hang start, pull until chin is over bar, lower with full control - 3 seconds down. Weighted pull-ups are arguably the best upper body exercise for building a wide, powerful back.

Dragon Flags

Core - Advanced Abs

3 Sets5-8 Reps90s Rest
Lie on a bench, grip behind head. Lift your entire body up on your shoulders, keeping it straight like a plank, then lower slowly without touching the bench. Made famous by Bruce Lee. Requires exceptional core strength and body control.
